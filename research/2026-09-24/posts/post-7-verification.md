## Notes / What Was Dropped

- The "hit-and-run requires physical contact or other proof" column was **not** added as a full table column across all 14 states. Only California and Georgia were verified against official statute text within the research budget; per the task's own rule ("If more than 4 states end up unverified for a column, drop that column"), this was kept as narrative text with two clearly-sourced examples instead of a table column implying coverage of all 14 states.
- No uninsured-driver population statistics, "typical UM settlement" figures, or average payout percentages were used anywhere in the post — no government-sourced statistic of this kind was found/verified, so per house rules none was invented or included.
- Pennsylvania's and Washington's UM statutes don't set a fixed dollar floor independent of the liability limits selected; this is stated as such in the post rather than inventing a number.
- Nevada is flagged in the post as an opt-in exception (insurer must offer, but coverage is only added if the insured affirmatively requests it in writing) rather than the more common opt-out pattern — this distinction is directly supported by the NRS 687B.145 quote above.

## Reviewer corrections (Sep 27, 2026)
- Arizona: A.R.S. 20-259.01(A) requires UM be offered "in limits not less than the liability limits for bodily injury or death contained within the policy"; the choice is recorded on the declarations page. Cell reworded.
- Florida: § 627.727(2) says UM limits are "not less than the limits of bodily injury liability insurance purchased ... or such lower limit ... as may be selected by the named insured." Cell now mentions the lower-limit option.
- Nevada: the current official NRS 687B.145(2) (amended 2015, 2021) requires insurers to offer UM/UIM "on a form approved by the Commissioner ... in an amount equal to the limits of coverage for bodily injury." The "request in writing" language applies to replacement policies only, so the draft's "opt-in" claim was wrong and has been removed. Source switched from the 2013 Justia copy to leg.state.nv.us.
